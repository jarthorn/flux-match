Flux Match

Flux Match is a simple dispatch model that simulates variable generation (wind, solar, and batteries) against historical hourly electricity demand across Canadian balancing areas. Generation is based on a hypothetical allocation of sites chosen from map grid cells; the model measures how well those choices cover real demand curves. It is deliberately simplistic—it excludes economics, grid connection costs, and other real-world constraints—and is meant for exploring site-selection strategies rather than producing operational forecasts.

Demand and capacity-factor data come from the CODERS database. Source code is on GitHub.

Results

  • Varying VRE capacity ratios

    An experiment that varies solar and wind nameplate capacity in each balancing area (as a ratio of average hourly demand) and plots the effect on net demand, curtailment, and satisfied demand hours, using a greedy annual-capacity site selection strategy.

  • Varying BESS capacity

    An experiment that varies battery energy storage (BESS) capacity in each balancing area (as a ratio of average hourly demand) and plots the effect on net demand, curtailment, and satisfied demand hours.

  • Hourly wind capacity factor, 2021

    An interactive map of Canada showing hourly wind capacity factors for every grid cell over a full year (8760 hours). Cells are coloured from pale blue (0) to deep red (1.0); play, scrub, and step through the year with the built-in controls.

    The same style of interactive map for solar capacity factors: hourly values for each Canadian grid cell across 2021, with play/pause, speed control, and a year scrubber.
