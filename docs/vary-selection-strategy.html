Flux Match Experiment: Varying Site Selection Strategy

In this experiment, wind, solar, and battery capacity are held fixed, and only the site selection strategy changes. The charts below show net demand, satisfied demand hours, and curtailment in each balancing area. Results are calculated using Flux Match, a very simple dispatch model for the Canadian electricity system. Demand and solar capacity factors are taken from the CODERS database; wind capacity factors are from renewables.ninja.

Greedy
Within each balancing area, cells are filled in rank order up to 2500 MW per cell until the wind and solar targets are met. This run ranks cells by annual capacity factor. It is the baseline: the strongest cells are taken first, with no attempt to separate sites geographically or to match their hourly profiles.
Cluster
Each balancing area's cells are split into 10 geographic clusters with K-means, seeded with 192837 and restarted 10 times. Coordinates are projected before clustering so a degree of longitude is not treated as equal to a degree of latitude. Wind and solar are then filled separately, in rounds. Each round takes the best remaining cell by annual capacity factor from every cluster and sets the rest of that cluster aside until the next round. Nearby cells are assumed to have similar hourly output, so spreading picks across clusters is meant to reduce that correlation.
TVCI
Total Variation Complementarity Index. Wind and solar are selected separately in each balancing area. If N sites are needed to meet the target at 2500 MW per cell, the candidate pool is the top 2N cells by annual capacity factor. The strongest candidate is taken first. Each later site is the remaining candidate whose hourly profile has the highest complementarity index against the sum of hourly capacity factors of the sites already chosen, so a new site is preferred when it generates in hours the earlier sites do not.
Wind-TVCI
Solar is placed first with Greedy, ranked by annual capacity. Wind is then chosen with TVCI. The complementarity baseline starts as the hourly capacity factors of the solar already placed in that balancing area, so every wind site, including the first, is chosen to complement that solar as well as any wind already picked. The wind candidate pool uses the same multiple of 2. An area with no solar falls back to ordinary TVCI for wind.

On the national totals, TVCI has the lowest net demand (8.8%) and the highest share of hours with demand satisfied (85.7%). Wind-TVCI is almost the same (8.82% net demand, 85.61% satisfied hours). Greedy is close on satisfied hours (85.63%) but leaves more net demand (9.37%) and the most curtailment (23.12%). Cluster is the outlier: the highest net demand (9.89%), the fewest satisfied hours (83.87%), and the lowest curtailment (16.63%).
