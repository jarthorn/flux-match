Flux Match Experiment: Varying BESS Capacity

In this experiment, we vary battery energy storage (BESS) capacity in each balancing area across Canada, and observe the impact on net demand, curtailment, and satisfied demand hours. The BESS capacity ratio is the ratio of battery energy capacity to the area's average daily demand. Battery power rating is capacity divided by duration (hours). Wind and solar capacity ratios are held fixed. Results are averaged across all balancing areas in Canada. Results are calculated using Flux Match, a very simple dispatch model for the Canadian electricity system. Demand and solar capacity factors are taken from the CODERS database; wind capacity factors are from renewables.ninja.

Duration was also varied (4, 12, and 20 h) in this experiment, but with constant capacity. As a result, the duration increase was offset by an equivalent decrease in power rating (doubling duration requires dropping the power rating by half). Because of this, increasing storage duration significantly decreased BESS effectiveness. At 20 hours, satisfied demand hours was 6% lower compared to 4 hour storage. In much of Canada, the effectiveness of BESS storage comes from shifting the solar peak generation to match the demand peak that comes later in the afternoon. The charts below show results for a 4-hour duration only.

The results show a clear improvement in net demand, curtailment, and satisfied demand hours as BESS capacity rises from 0.5 toward about 1.5–2. With higher BESS capacity beyond that, there is a significant dropoff in improvement. This suggests that building some initial BESS capacity is quite important for a VRE-dominated system, but once there is enough BESS capacity to meet average daily demand, further investment may be better spent on increasing VRE capacity than on BESS capacity.
