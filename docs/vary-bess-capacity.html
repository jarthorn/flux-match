Flux Match Experiment: Varying BESS Capacity

In this experiment, we vary battery energy storage (BESS) capacity in each balancing area across Canada, and observe the impact on net demand, curtailment, and satisfied demand hours. The BESS capacity ratio is the ratio of battery energy capacity to the area's average daily demand. Battery power rating is capacity divided by duration (hours). Wind and solar capacity ratios are held fixed. Results are averaged across all balancing areas in Canada. Results are calculated using Flux Match, a very simple dispatch model for the Canadian electricity system. Demand data, and solar/wind capacity factors, are taken from the CODERS database.

Duration was also varied (4–20 h) in this experiment, but with constant capacity. As a result, the duration increase was offset by an equivalent decrease on power rating (doubling duration requires dropping the power rating by half). Because of this, storage duration increase has a negligible effect on net demand, curtailment, and satisfied demand hours. The charts below show results for a 4-hour duration only.

The results show a dramatic improvement in net demand, curtailment, and satisfied demand hours when BESS capacity goes from 0 to 1. With higher BESS capacity beyond 1.0, there is a signnificant dropoff in improvement. This suggests that building some initial BESS capacity is quite important for a VRE-dominated system, but once there is enough BESS capacity to meet average daily demand, further investment may be better spent on increasing VRE capacity than on BESS capacity.
