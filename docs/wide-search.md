# Overview
In this project, we are implementing and evaluating algorithms that select sites for renewable energy projects (wind and solar). A key characteristic we are looking for is complementarity, so that drops in generation at one resource are complemented by generation at another resource. We should do a literature search across different technical domains where anti-correlation or complementarity between sets of time-series data is sought out. Some possible domains:
- Financial algorithms that seek to hedge volatility across multiple investments by finding complementary investments
- Structural engineering. If one structural member experiences high stress, they look for complementary load-bearing shifts in adjacent structural ribs or dampening systems to ensure safety margins are maintained.
- Earth-system models look at how different environmental compartments (atmosphere, hydrosphere, cryosphere, and biosphere) interact over varying time scales. For instance, carbon uptake by terrestrial vegetation is evaluated in complementarity with oceanic absorption and atmospheric accumulation
- Acoustics, perhaps in noise cancelation or filtering, where complementarity is used to clean or negate sound waves across multiple inputs

A good energy industry survey on complementarity is found in a literature review from 2020 ( [Jurasz et al](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0038092X19311831)). Energy mostly uses Pearson/Kendall correlation, the Beluco index, compromise programming, and total variation indices, almost always pairwise. That's the bar for "novel" below.

The following is a summary from brainstorming discussion with Claude Fable 5.1. For each technical domain, this includes a summary of the idea each domain contributes, and how it maps to the problem in flux-match. These ideas require further vetting for correctness and applicability.

## How flux-match maps onto the general problem

Choose \(k\) series from \(n\) candidates (8760-hour CF profiles, capped capacity each) so the *aggregate* best covers a *target* (demand) with a few hours of storage slack. Four properties matter when borrowing from other fields: (1) it's cardinality-constrained subset selection, (2) the objective is about the aggregate's shortfall vs. a target, not pairwise anti-correlation, (3) only low-generation / high-net-demand hours really matter, and (4) storage tolerates lags of a few hours. The current TVCI greedy addresses (1) only partly and (2)–(4) not at all, which is where the ideas below come in.

## Domains with a near-identical problem (direct transplants)

**Optical ground-station network design (satcom).** Selecting ground stations whose cloud cover is uncorrelated so at least one has clear sky. [Fuchs & Moll (DLR)](https://doi.org/10.1364/jocn.7.001148) pre-filter candidates by single-site availability × low mean pairwise correlation, then do combinatorial search on the reduced set; the [Australasian FSOC study](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.09248) uses an iterative heuristic minimizing a weighted norm of (cloud cover, correlation with already-selected sites), a Monte Carlo sampler for the *joint* outage distribution of a correlated network, and notes both positive *and* negative correlation can hurt. Essentially solar siting under a different name; the pre-filter + joint-outage-probability framing is cleaner than what energy typically does.

**Cloud-RAN complementary base-station clustering.** [Chen et al.](https://uwaterloo.ca/geospatial-intelligence/sites/default/files/uploads/files/2018-chen-yang-zhang-wang-li-nguyen-jnca.pdf) group cells so one baseband pool serves traffic peaks that occur at different times. Two transferable pieces: a **peak-hour entropy metric** (collect each series' top-\(m\) peak timestamps; Shannon entropy of the pooled peak-hour distribution; higher = more complementary), multiplied by a capacity-utility term, and a distance-constrained community-detection clustering on a complementarity-weighted graph. Peak-time entropy is set-wise (not pairwise), cheap, and I didn't find it in energy siting.

**Correlation-aware VM placement.** [CompVM](https://www.cs.virginia.edu/~hs6ms/publishedPaper/Journal/2018/CompVM-TON-2018.pdf) and the [INRIA periodic-demand packing work](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-48096-0_12) place workloads so the sum at *every timestep* stays under capacity. Key twist: CompVM picks the host whose **residual capacity series** is most anti-correlated with the new VM, i.e. it correlates against the *residual*, not against the other VMs. For flux-match: score candidates against the current net-demand residual, not against the average of selected sites.

**Hydrometric / sensor network design.** Classic information-theoretic selection: [MIMR](https://doi.org/10.1029/2011wr011251) (max joint entropy, max transinformation, min total correlation, greedy ranking), [Krause et al.](http://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume9/krause08a/krause08a.pdf) (mutual information is submodular, so greedy gets \((1-1/e)\) of optimal). Two cautions relevant to your TVCI greedy: [Foroozand & Weijs 2021](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-25-831-2021) argue explicit redundancy penalties are unnecessary when the set objective already accounts for them and introduce "greedy drop" (backward elimination from the full set), and they show no greedy scheme is guaranteed optimal; TVCI against a running average has no submodularity guarantee either.

## Different objective functions worth borrowing

**Ecology portfolio effect (community stability).** The [Loreau & de Mazancourt synchrony index](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/589746) \(\varphi = \mathrm{Var}(\sum_i x_i) / (\sum_i \sigma_i)^2\) ranges 0 (perfect asynchrony) to 1 and is set-wise and scale-free. [Thibaut & Connolly](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3588152/) show aggregate CV \(= \sqrt{\varphi}\times\) (abundance-weighted mean site CV): aggregate variability is synchrony × average individual variability, so a steady, moderately correlated site can beat a volatile, anti-correlated one. The Gross et al. metric (in R `codyn`) is the mean over sites of \(\mathrm{corr}(x_i, \sum_{j\neq i} x_j)\), which is exactly your "correlate with the rest of the portfolio" idea with a theoretical footing and a natural leave-one-out form for swap/prune steps. Confirmed empirically in [salmon](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature09060) and [national crop production](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-020-2965-6) ("asynchrony predicts stability better than diversity").

**Catastrophe (re)insurance and cat bonds.** [Woo](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2001/insurance01/woo.PDF) frames diversification in *event space*: a new bond should be "orthogonal" in the sense that no single hazard event hits both it and the existing book. [Castella & Chiolero](https://actuaries.org/app/uploads/2025/07/Castella_Chiolero_Paper_Zurich2005.pdf) note event-induced dependence differs from Gaussian correlation and tail dependence must be tested, not assumed. Translation: define "low-generation events" per site (e.g. CF below a threshold for ≥ 6 h) and select sites whose event sets overlap least.

**Climate networks: event synchronization.** This supplies the metric for the above. [Quiroga's event synchronization](https://www.atmos.colostate.edu/~iebert/PAPERS/CI2018_paper_12.pdf) counts extreme events at two locations that co-occur within a window \(\tau\) (with a directed variant giving lead/lag). Setting \(\tau\) ≈ `bess_duration` makes the metric storage-aware and tail-focused, two things Pearson/TVCI on full series are not. Climate networks also use community detection to find regions that behave as a block, which is a principled replacement for geographic k-means in your cluster strategy.

**Finance beyond Markowitz.** Cardinality-constrained min-variance is exactly your problem ([Bertsimas & Cory-Wright](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/ijoc.2021.1127); [Boolean relaxation](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.10099) for scale). More interesting: [Choueifaty's Most Diversified Portfolio](https://tobam.fr/wp-content/uploads/2014/12/04.2013_JIS_TOBAM-Properties-of-the-Most-Diversified-Portfolio.pdf) has the property that every excluded asset is *more* correlated with the portfolio than any included asset, which gives a clean swap/termination test. [Hierarchical Risk Parity](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2708678) clusters on correlation distance and allocates recursively, deliberately avoiding covariance inversion because in-sample correlation optimizers are unstable out-of-sample (a warning for any single-CF-year optimization). Minimum tail-dependence portfolios use lower-tail copula dependence instead of correlation, focusing on joint bad periods. CVaR/expected-shortfall minimization has an LP formulation that directly minimizes shortfall in the worst hours.

**Conservation planning ("complementarity" is their term).** [Margules & Pressey](https://www.nature.com/articles/35012251): a site's complementarity is the number of *not-yet-represented* features it adds, recalculated after every selection; a richness ranking (your greedy baseline) is known to be inefficient. Translation: features = hour (or hour-of-day × month) bins with unmet demand; complementarity = unmet demand a site covers. This is maximum coverage, submodular, with greedy guarantees. Their **irreplaceability** measure (fraction of near-optimal portfolios that include a site) is a useful robustness output for site ranking.

**Sparse approximation / multi-reference active noise control.** Both arrive at the same algorithm from different directions. [Orthogonal Matching Pursuit](https://tisp.indigits.com/sparse_approx/omp_sa) selects the atom most correlated with the current residual then refits all selected atoms by least squares; [non-negative OMP](https://doi.org/10.1109/tsp.2019.2943225) adds non-negativity, which maps to capacity allocations (and your per-cell cap is just a box constraint on the NNLS). In ANC, reference sensors are chosen by greedy forward selection on *multiple coherence* with the error signal, dropping references whose information is already explained ([Euronoise 2026](https://dael.euracoustics.org/confs/fa2026/data/articles/000416.pdf)), or by Boolean programming minimizing output noise power ([EUSIPCO 2025](https://eusipco2025.org/wp-content/uploads/pdfs/0000376.pdf)). "Error signal" = demand; this is target-matching rather than mutual anti-correlation.

**Structural engineering (your example).** Redundancy here is quantified by member-removal analysis: demand-to-capacity ratio after removing a critical member ([alternate load path framework](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/12/10/1597)), or \(\det(K_{damaged})/\det(K_{intact})\) ([robustness indices](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/10/12/213)). It's an evaluation idea rather than a selection algorithm: report N-1 robustness of a portfolio (shortfall when any single site is removed or has its worst week). Portfolios chosen purely for anti-correlation can be fragile to losing one site.

**Earth-system carbon sinks (your example).** [Zeng et al.](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2004GB002273) and [Doney et al.](https://doi.org/10.1175/jcli3783.1) show land and ocean fluxes are out of phase, but the compensation is *frequency-dependent* (strongly anti-correlated at >20-yr periods, weaker at ENSO band) and involves lead/lag. The method lesson is band-wise, lag-aware anti-correlation (wavelet/spectral coherence). For you: the battery handles the diurnal band, so complementarity should be weighted toward synoptic (3–10 day) and seasonal bands.

## Algorithmic machinery from other fields

**QR column pivoting / log-det volume.** [Manohar, Brunton & Kutz](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.07569) select sensors via pivoted QR on a data matrix; it picks the most linearly independent columns in one factorization, is hierarchical (first \(p\) pivots are the answer for any \(p\)), and is far cheaper than \(O(n^2)\) TVCI. Determinantal point processes ([constrained DPP MAP](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1607.01551)) sample subsets with probability ∝ Gram determinant, with a quality term to favor high-CF sites; k-DPP sampling gives a cheap way to generate many diverse near-optimal portfolios for comparison.

**Exchange (swap) local search.** [Fedorov/DETMAX](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/AlgDesign/html/Federov.html) from optimal experimental design, also used for [seismic network design](https://doi.org/10.1785/bssa0800010187): start from a greedy solution, repeatedly swap one selected for one candidate if the objective improves, with rank-one determinant updates and random restarts. Typically a large improvement over pure greedy at modest cost. Marxan (conservation) uses simulated annealing for the same reason; the [surveillance-network paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-20399-3) optimizes directly on a networked simulation with SA, which maps to running SA on your dispatch metric after screening.

**Ensemble pruning (ML).** "Overproduce and choose" is your 2N candidate pool. Two findings transfer: [Brown & Kuncheva's good vs. bad diversity](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13748-014-0042-9): diversity only helps on the *errors*, so complementarity should be weighted by hours of positive net demand. And pruning is validated on held-out data; with one CF year you risk fitting weather noise, so hold out a year or use block bootstrap.

**Temporal niche overlap (ecology).** [Pianka/Czekanowski overlap](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/rosario/rosario.pdf) on circular time, and crucially the [ROSARIO null model](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2041-210x.2010.00031.x): cyclically shift each series to build a null distribution that preserves autocorrelation. Applied to TVCI, this tells you whether a measured complementarity is better than chance for series with strong diurnal/seasonal cycles, which plain correlation cannot.

**Antenna selection diversity / risk pooling.** Outage probability \(=P(\text{all branches below threshold})\), which decays geometrically only if branches are decorrelated (spacing > 0.3λ in [antenna diversity](https://ecen665web.groups.et.byu.net/notes/LN%20Diversity.pdf)). [Eppen's pooling result](https://engineering.lehigh.edu/sites/engineering.lehigh.edu/files/_DEPARTMENTS/ise/pdf/tech-papers/11/11w_002.pdf): pooled SD \(= \sigma\sqrt{n(1+(n-1)\bar\rho)}\), so at scale only the *mean* pairwise correlation matters and benefits saturate; [supplier-yield work](https://doi.org/10.1287/msom.2020.0918) shows adding a weaker but correlated source can reduce combined reliability. Cheap translations: a minimum-separation pre-filter derived from the empirical decorrelation length of wind CF, and "fraction of hours all selected sites are below threshold" as a portfolio metric.

## Suggested priorities for flux-match

Highest expected value per unit effort, and all plausibly untried in energy siting:

1. **Residual-driven selection** (OMP / conservation complementarity / CompVM): score candidates against the current net-demand residual (optionally after a cheap battery pass) instead of against the average of selected sites. Directly targets your objective and reuses the existing greedy loop.
2. **Exchange local search** after any greedy: swap-improve on the dispatch metric with restarts.
3. **Event synchronization on low-CF events** with window \(\tau\) = battery duration, as a storage-aware, tail-focused complementarity metric alongside TVCI.
4. **Loreau \(\varphi\)** and **peak-hour entropy** as set-wise portfolio objectives (compare against pairwise TVCI).
5. **QR pivoting** as a fast baseline, and **Fuchs–Moll style pre-filtering** (availability × decorrelation) to shrink the candidate pool.
6. Evaluation add-ons: N-1 robustness, irreplaceability across near-optimal portfolios, cyclic-shift null model for TVCI significance, held-out CF year.
